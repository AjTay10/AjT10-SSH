Reach fell 64% on one platform. The content never changed.

A full run of the analytics pipeline over three raw platform exports — three different column vocabularies, three different encodings — from raw file to diagnosis, with every number produced by the tools rather than asserted.

90 days · Mar–May 2026 120 posts · 3 platforms 9 tools · stdlib only 0 network calls
Finding

YouTube impressions per post dropped 64% on 12 May and did not recover. Engagement rate held flat across the same boundary, and no corresponding break appears on TikTok within a week either side.

Flat engagement with collapsed reach is a distribution failure, not a content failure — and the two lead to completely different investigations. Check instrumentation and platform status before touching the content.

The run

Four commands. Each one is shown with its actual output — nothing below is reconstructed or tidied.

01

Inspect before trusting anything

Platform exports name the same quantity differently and quietly change format between versions. The mapping gets confirmed before a single number is computed.

Note the two hard cases. Video publish time resolves to date, not post_id — a naive substring match reads the “id” inside “v-id-eo”. And Watch time (hours) is converted to seconds on the way in, so 9.3 becomes 33,480 rather than being silently off by 3,600×.
02

Normalize three platforms into one schema

The Instagram file came out of a spreadsheet round-trip as cp1252. That decode error is a ValueError, not an OSError, so it slips past the usual handler and reaches the user as a traceback — the reader falls back through encodings and says which one it used.

python3 tools/social_ingest.py \
    --csv youtube_studio_export.csv --platform youtube \
    --csv tiktok_export.csv         --platform tiktok \
    --csv instagram_export.csv      --platform instagram \
    --out normalized.csv --summary
note: instagram_export.csv is not UTF-8; read it as cp1252. Check any accented characters in the output.
normalized.csv: 120 rows
note: youtube_studio_export.csv: 1 column(s) not mapped and dropped: Impressions click-through rate (%)
note: youtube: Views are counted after ~30s or full watch on Shorts; impressions here are thumbnail impressions, not views.
note: tiktok: A 'view' is counted at 0s (immediate autoplay). TikTok views are not comparable to YouTube views — never chart them on one axis.
note: instagram: Reach is unique accounts; impressions can be many per account. Reels 'plays' start at ~1s.

Do not rank platforms on this ER column alone — the denominators are defined differently per platform (see notes).
The tool argues with its own output. That last line is printed by the normalizer itself. TikTok’s 12.80% against YouTube’s 0.34% is not a quality gap — a TikTok view counts at 0 seconds while a YouTube impression is a thumbnail render. Ranking those against each other produces a conclusion that is wrong rather than merely imprecise.
03

Ask whether anything actually broke

The baseline is a trailing Theil–Sen trend fit, not a rolling average, so ordinary growth is not reported as a run of spikes. Scoring uses median absolute deviation, so one viral post cannot widen the band and hide every real problem after it.

python3 tools/anomaly.py --csv youtube.csv --date date --value reach --window 12

2026-05-18        98,296.00    220,333.33   -5.93  drop

1 sustained level shift(s) — these matter more than spikes, and no daily-spike alert would have caught them:
  2026-05-12 → 2026-05-18 (3 pts)  down  (cusum -9.5)
The changepoints are the finding, not the spikes. A single bad day is usually noise. A level that shifts and stays shifted is the thing that shows up a quarter later as “growth stalled” — and it never trips a threshold alert, because no individual day is unusual enough.
04

Split distribution failure from content failure

The two produce identical-looking charts and require opposite responses. The discriminator is whether engagement rate moved with reach.

Reach collapsed; the rate at which the people who saw it engaged did not. The audience reacted to this content exactly as it always had — there were simply far fewer of them. 6 posts after the break is a thin sample, so this is a strong indication rather than a settled result.

What the series look like

Two platforms, two charts — deliberately not one. Putting YouTube impressions and TikTok views on a shared axis would imply a comparison the underlying definitions do not support.

Every chart is a self-contained SVG with no external requests — no CDN, no webfont, no image fetch. Colors are emitted as CSS custom properties with a second palette under prefers-color-scheme, which is why these follow your theme.

A result that looks like a win

Alongside the reach question, a new post format was tested against the existing one. It converted at 4.81% against 3.48% — a 38% relative lift, and the kind of number that gets a format rolled out everywhere.

python3 tools/abtest.py compare --a 41/1180 --b 58/1205
A    3.475%        41/1,180     [2.57%, 4.68%]
B    4.813%        58/1,205     [3.74%, 6.17%]

absolute difference  +1.339 pp  [-0.259, +2.936] at 95%
relative lift        +38.5%
two-sided p          0.1013   (z = +1.639)

verdict: NOT significant. The interval contains zero — B may be worse. Do not ship on this.
python3 tools/abtest.py bayes --a 41/1180 --b 58/1205

P(B > A)                 94.5%
expected loss if B ships 0.0177 pp
expected gain if B ships 1.3526 pp

read: inconclusive. The honest move is more data or a bigger swing — not a rerun with a new metric.
Both readings are shown because they answer different questions. The frequentist test says the difference has not been demonstrated. The Bayesian read says B is probably better and the downside of being wrong is small. For a reversible change that combination argues for shipping; for an irreversible one it argues for more data. The framework should follow the reversibility of the decision — and neither result supports the headline “+38%”.

Five things the pipeline caught that a direct read would not

MappingA date column read as an ID
“Video publish time” contains the letters id inside “video”. Substring matching assigns it to post_id, the real date column goes unmapped, and every time series silently sorts by nothing.
UnitsWatch time off by a factor of 3,600
YouTube exports hours; the schema stores seconds. Copying the number through produces a plausible-looking figure that is wrong by three orders of magnitude — the kind of error that survives review because nobody has an intuition for the correct value.
EncodingA spreadsheet round-trip in cp1252
Excel writes cp1252 constantly. UnicodeDecodeError subclasses ValueError, so it slips past except OSError and surfaces as a raw traceback on the exact files the tool exists to read.
BaselineGrowth misreported as anomalies
A rolling-median baseline sits permanently below a trending series, so a healthy account generates a continuous run of false spikes and the alert gets muted within a week. Fitting slope first means a flag means “this broke from its own trajectory”.
ComparisonA cross-platform ranking that means nothing
12.80% against 0.34% is a definitional artifact of when each platform starts counting, not a quality gap. The normalizer prints that warning next to the numbers rather than letting the table stand alone.
