# Outreach templates

Rules that apply to all of them:

- **Lead with a specific finding, never with a service.** "I noticed X about
  your listing" outperforms "I offer diligence services" by a margin that makes
  the comparison pointless.
- **Under 120 words.** Every sentence past that reduces reply rate.
- **One ask, and make it small.** "Want the rest?" beats "book a call".
- **Never fabricate the finding.** If you have not actually looked, do not
  claim to have. This entire business is accuracy; opening with a bluff kills it
  on contact.
- **No follow-up beyond two.** Send, wait 4 days, one follow-up, stop.

---

## 1. Seller — active listing, you have looked at the public numbers

> **Subject:** the traffic concentration in your listing
>
> Hi {name} — I look at the numbers behind content businesses for a living.
>
> Your listing shows {N} pages, but from the public figures the top few look
> like they carry most of the traffic. A buyer will run that number in their
> first hour and it becomes a discount conversation you're having on the back
> foot.
>
> I ran a fuller version — concentration, whether recent content is replacing
> old content, and whether the trend survives a changepoint test. Happy to send
> it over, no charge, and you can decide if it's worth pre-empting.
>
> {signature}

**Why this works:** it gives away the finding, names the mechanism (a discount
conversation), and asks for nothing. If they reply, they have self-identified.

## 2. Seller — pre-listing, they have said publicly they are thinking of selling

> **Subject:** before you list
>
> Hi {name} — saw you're considering selling {asset}.
>
> The three things buyers discount hardest are concentration, whether recent
> work still performs, and whether the growth trend survives scrutiny. Most
> listings get hit on at least one, and the seller finds out during
> negotiation rather than before it.
>
> I run those three checks and write up what the numbers support. If you send a
> page-level export and a monthly series I'll do the first pass free — it takes
> me about an hour and you'll know what you're walking into.
>
> {signature}

---

## 3. Broker or marketplace — the highest-value relationship

> **Subject:** metric analysis for your $50k+ listings
>
> Hi {name} — I do statistical diligence on content and creator businesses:
> concentration, decay, and whether a growth trend survives a changepoint test.
> It's the analytical layer under the usual traffic and revenue verification,
> not a replacement for it.
>
> Two ways it might be useful to you: sellers who pre-empt the obvious
> objections close faster, and listings with a clean analytical pack attract
> better-qualified buyers.
>
> I'll do one of your current listings free so you can judge the output. If
> it's useful, I'm happy to work on referral terms.
>
> {signature}

**Note:** offering revenue share unprompted is correct here. Their deal flow is
worth far more than the margin at this stage.

## 4. Buyer — off-marketplace only

Do not send this to buyers on a marketplace that sells its own diligence. You
lose that comparison and it makes you look unaware.

> **Subject:** what that growth chart rests on
>
> Hi {name} — if you're evaluating {asset}, one thing worth checking before you
> get to price: how much of the traffic sits in the top three pages, and
> whether anything published in the last 18 months is performing.
>
> I run that analysis — concentration, decay, and whether the trend survives a
> changepoint test — and write up what the numbers do and don't support. Two
> days, fixed fee, and it explicitly isn't a valuation.
>
> Want a sample from a previous (anonymized) one?
>
> {signature}

---

## 5. The free sample — what you actually send

Not the full report. One page:

1. **One finding**, stated in a sentence, with the number.
2. **The chart** that shows it (`chartkit` hbar or line).
3. **One line on what it means for them** — a discount conversation, a
   question they will be asked, a risk they should price.
4. **What you could not determine** from public data alone.
5. One sentence offering the full version.

Cap it at 20 minutes of work. If the free sample takes an hour you cannot
afford to send 20 of them, and 20 is the number that produces a signal.

---

## 6. Reply to "what's it worth?"

You will get this every time. The answer is fixed:

> That's not what I do — I don't give valuations, and anyone doing this
> analysis who also quotes you a price has a conflict. What I can tell you is
> what the numbers support and what they can't establish, which is usually the
> part that moves a negotiation anyway.

Saying this costs you nothing and buys the credibility the whole thing runs on.

---

## 7. Reply to "why should I trust you?"

> Fair. I don't have a track record with you, so don't take my word for
> anything — the report shows the arithmetic and names its own limits, and the
> tooling is public at {repo}. Judge the free sample and decide from there.

Never oversell here. The work is the credential; pointing at it is stronger
than any claim about yourself.
