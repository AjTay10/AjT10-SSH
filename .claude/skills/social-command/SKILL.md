## The three decisions that determine the outcome

Everything else is execution detail. Get these wrong and no amount of posting
fixes it.

**1. One platform, not five.** The single most common failure is spreading
thin across every platform at once, producing mediocre native content on all
of them and mastering none. Each platform is a different medium with a
different craft — the skill does not transfer as much as it appears to.

Pick one primary platform on two criteria: *where the audience already is*,
and *which format the creator can sustain*. The second matters more than
people accept. A person who hates being on camera will not out-execute on
TikTok, and no strategy survives that.

Add a second platform only when the first is working, and add it as
*distribution of the same work* (see `content-atomizer`), not as a second
production line.

**2. A repeatable format, not a stream of ideas.** Audiences subscribe to a
promise, and the algorithm rewards a signal it can categorize. "Whatever I
feel like" fails both.

A format is: a fixed subject, a fixed structure, and a fixed payoff.
"Teardown of one landing page, weekly" is a format. "Marketing tips" is a
topic and will not compound.

Ship 10 in the same format before evaluating it. Fewer than 10 and you are
reading noise — the base rate for a single post outperforming an account's
median is roughly 1 in 20.

**3. An owned destination.** Every platform audience is rented, and the rent
can be raised or the account removed without appeal or explanation. Reach
that does not convert to something you own — an email list, primarily — is
a number that can go to zero.

The conversion metric that matters is not follower count. It is
**followers → owned contacts**, and it is usually under 1%.

## The content system

```
PILLARS       3–5 recurring subjects. Anything outside them dilutes the
              promise and confuses categorization.
FORMATS       2–3 repeatable structures per pillar.
CADENCE       What is sustainable for 6 months at your worst week, not your
              best. Consistency beats frequency; both lose to quality when
              distribution is ranked per-post.
RATIO         Roughly 70% serves the audience, 20% builds credibility,
              10% asks for something. The ask fails without the other 90%.
```

## Sequencing a campaign

Most campaigns fail by launching cold to an audience that has no context.

```
T−21   Seed the problem. Content about the pain, no product mentioned.
       You are building the audience that will care, and testing which
       framing of the problem lands.
T−7    Show the process. Behind the scenes, the build, the decision.
       This is what earns the right to sell later.
T−1    Announce mechanics. What, when, where. No persuasion yet.
T=0    Launch. The lightest lift of the whole campaign if the run-up worked.
T+1..7 Proof, not promotion. Results, responses, objections answered.
T+14   Post-mortem, published if it went badly — this outperforms the
       launch post remarkably often and costs nothing to write.
```

The T−21 window is what nearly everyone skips, and it is the one that
determines whether T=0 lands at all.

## Measure these four, ignore the rest

```
REACH        impressions or video views      — did distribution happen?
RESONANCE    engagement rate per post        — did it land with who saw it?
CAPTURE      followers or subscribers gained — did it compound?
CONVERSION   owned contacts or revenue       — did it matter?
```

A post can win one and lose the others; each failure means something specific.
High reach with low resonance means the hook oversold the content. High
resonance with low reach means good content the algorithm did not distribute —
usually a categorization or a timing problem, not a quality one. Everything
high except conversion means you built an audience for a different product
than the one you sell.

Never optimize follower count directly. It is the easiest of these to inflate
and the least connected to anything.

## Honest constraints

- **Virality is an outcome, never a plan.** Any strategy that requires it has
  no base rate to stand on. Build for the median post.
- **Reach is rented and repossessed without notice.** Plan for a platform
  going to zero; it happens to somebody every year.
- **Follower counts are noisy and partly purchasable.** Engagement rate at a
  given follower count is far more informative, including when evaluating
  someone else's account.
- **Ranked feeds allocate per post.** More posts split the same attention;
  frequency is not free. See `contrarian-scan` on this specific belief.
