# Contrarian scan

Being different is easy and usually stupid. The valuable position is
**correct and non-consensus** — where the crowd believes something that is
false or merely unexamined, and you can demonstrate it.

Three of the four quadrants are worthless. Only one pays:

```
                 consensus            non-consensus
correct       no edge (crowded)    ◀── the only paying square
wrong         crowded and wrong    lonely and wrong (most contrarians)
```

Most people who set out to be contrarian land in the bottom right, where the
crowd is right and they are alone. The scan exists to keep you out of it.

## Procedure

**1. Write the consensus explicitly.** List 8–12 beliefs everyone in the space
holds. The hardest part: consensus is invisible from inside it. Find it by
reading the *advice*, not the analysis — the things repeated in every
beginner guide, every conference talk, every "10 tips" post.

Prompt yourself with: "What does every single account in this niche do the
same way, without ever explaining why?"

**2. For each belief, ask which of four things it is.**

| Type | Test | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **True and load-bearing** | evidence exists, mechanism is clear | leave it alone |
| **True then, false now** | was validated under conditions that changed | ← best opportunities |
| **True for them, false for you** | depends on scale, capital, or audience you lack or exceed | ← second best |
| **Never tested, just repeated** | trace it and you find no origin, only citation | ← cheapest to test |

Category two is where most real edges live. Platform mechanics, distribution
costs, and tooling change constantly; the advice does not. Anything phrased as
a universal rule that originated in specific 2019 conditions is a candidate.

**3. Trace the origin of the most-repeated belief.** Follow the citation chain
back. Very often it terminates at one blog post, one conference talk, or one
company's idiosyncratic experience that was never generalizable. When the
chain terminates in nothing, you have found an unpriced belief.

**4. Check whether the crowd is right anyway.** Mandatory, and the step people
skip. Most consensus is consensus because it is true and the mechanism is
sound. Ask: *what does the crowd know that makes this obviously correct?*
If you cannot answer, you have not understood the position well enough to
oppose it. If the answer is good, drop the candidate and move on — dropping
candidates here is the skill working, not failing.

**5. Convert survivors into a testable position.** Not "everyone is wrong
about X" but a claim with a mechanism and a cheap test.

## Output

```
CONSENSUS          "Post daily or the algorithm buries you."
TYPE               True then, false now
ORIGIN             Chronological-feed era, pre-2016. Traced to growth-hacking
                   posts written when the feed was time-ordered.
WHY IT PERSISTS    It is unfalsifiable from the inside: people who post daily
                   and grow attribute growth to frequency, and nobody runs
                   the control.
WHY IT MAY BE OFF  Ranked feeds distribute on per-post engagement rate.
                   Frequency raises the denominator; each post competes with
                   your own recent posts for the same audience's attention.
MECHANISM          If reach is allocated per-post by predicted ER, and ER is
                   diluted across more posts, then 3 strong posts can out-reach
                   9 weak ones. This is checkable, not rhetorical.
CHEAP TEST         4 weeks at 3 posts/week vs 4 weeks at 9. Compare total
                   reach and total engagement, not per-post averages.
IF RIGHT           2/3 less production for equal or better reach — which is
                   an advantage competitors cannot copy without believing it.
IF WRONG           One month of lower output. Small, recoverable.
CONFIDENCE         55%. Non-consensus but far from proven.
```

## Guardrails

- **Never adopt the position before the test.** The scan generates candidates,
  not conclusions. Confidence above 70% before testing means you fell in love.
- **Contrarian on mechanism, not on taste.** "Everyone uses bright thumbnails
  so I will use muted ones" is aesthetics wearing a strategy costume.
- **Check the survivorship story.** If the consensus is derived only from
  studying winners, it is probably describing correlates of success, not causes.
- **Beware the cheap inversion.** Reversing a true statement produces a false
  one, and it feels like insight. Every candidate needs a *mechanism* for why
  the reverse is true, not merely the observation that it is unpopular.
