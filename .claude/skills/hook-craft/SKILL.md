# Hook craft

The opening does one job: buy the next three seconds. It cannot sell the whole
piece, and trying is what makes hooks fail.

Nearly all "low reach" problems are hook problems. On interest-graph platforms
distribution is allocated on early retention, so a weak first three seconds
caps the post before the content has any chance to matter.

## Diagnose before you write

Check the retention curve or the drop-off:

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Cliff in first 3s | The hook did not register at all | New hook — visual and verbal simultaneously |
| Cliff at 5–15s | Hook wrote a check the content did not cash | Deliver a fragment of the payoff sooner |
| Steady decline | Normal. Do not "fix" it | Look at pacing only if steeper than peers |
| Cliff mid-piece | One specific segment is dead | Cut that segment next time |
| High CTR, low retention | Title/thumbnail mismatch — actively harmful | Align the promise; this trains the system against you |

The last row matters: on YouTube a high-CTR, low-retention video is worse
than a low-CTR one, because it teaches the system that your thumbnails
mislead.

## The mechanisms that work

There are only a handful, and they all create an *open loop* the brain wants
closed.

**1. Information gap.** State a fact that implies a question.
> "This channel gets 40% of its views from one video I almost deleted."

**2. Pattern interrupt.** Violate the expected opening of the format.
> Opening mid-sentence, mid-action, or on the result.

**3. Stakes.** Name what is lost if they leave.
> "If you're doing this in your first 90 days, you're capping your reach."

**4. Contradiction.** State something the audience believes and negate it —
only when you can actually support it.
> "Posting daily is why your reach is falling."

**5. Specificity.** A precise number is more credible and more interesting
than a rounded one.
> "$4,270 in 19 days" beats "thousands of dollars quickly."

**6. Named enemy.** A common frustration, stated precisely.
> "Every guide tells you to 'find your niche' and none of them say how."

**7. Immediate payoff.** Give the answer first, then explain it. Strongly
underused, and the most robust of all of these — because it is not a
technique, it is respecting the viewer's time.

## Rules

**Front-load the concrete noun.** The first two words should be the subject,
not a windup. "Retention editing" not "So today I want to talk about…"

**Cut the greeting.** "Hey guys, welcome back" is three seconds of nothing at
the exact moment distribution is decided. There is no version of this that
works.

**Visual and verbal must fire together.** On video the first frame is doing
as much work as the first sentence. A static talking head over a strong line
still loses.

**Match the promise to what you actually have.** A hook that oversells
produces the 5–15s cliff, which is worse than a weaker hook that lands.

**Second person, present tense.** "You're losing reach" not "Creators lose
reach."

**Cut every qualifier.** "Might", "could", "I think", "in my opinion" all
weaken the open loop. Keep the honesty in the content, not in the hook —
but do not make a claim you cannot support.

## Per-platform placement

| Platform | Where the hook lives | Budget |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok / Reels / Shorts | Frames 1–3, visual + audio + on-screen text | ~1.5s |
| YouTube long | Title + thumbnail pair, then first 30s | title ~60 chars visible |
| X | First line before truncation | ~40 chars |
| LinkedIn | First 2 lines before "see more" | ~210 chars |
| Email | Subject + preview text as one unit | ~40 + ~90 chars |
| Reddit | Title *is* the entire pitch | ≤ 300 chars |
| Pinterest | Image text + keyword-bearing title | image legible at thumbnail size |

## Generate 10, ship 1

Write ten hooks for every piece, using a *different mechanism* each time —
not ten variations of one. Ten phrasings of the same angle test nothing.

Then cut on:
1. Does it work with zero context? (Assume a stranger, mid-scroll)
2. Is it legible in under one second?
3. Can the content actually deliver it?
4. Would you stop for it? Honestly?

Cut 1 and 3 are where most hooks die.

## Thumbnails

One idea. Legible at 120px wide — test it at that size, not at full size.
Face with a clear single emotion outperforms a neutral one. Three words
maximum, and they must not repeat the title — the pair should create *one*
question together, not two competing ones.

If a thumbnail needs reading to be understood, it has already failed.

## Ethical line

Curiosity gaps are fine; lying is not, and it is also bad strategy. A hook
that misrepresents produces a click, an immediate exit, and a permanent
downweight from the ranking system. The system is measuring whether you
delivered, so deception is punished mechanically, not just morally.

Never fabricate a number, a result, or a quote for a hook. See
`compliance-guard` for disclosure requirements on anything promotional.
