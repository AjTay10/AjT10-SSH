# Comment ops

Replies are not overhead. On most platforms early reply activity is a ranking
input, so the first hour in the comments does more for distribution than any
amount of post-scheduling. And the comment section is where the next ten
pieces of content are written for you, by the people who want them.

## Triage, in priority order

When a post takes off, work the comments in this order:

1. **Questions** — answer first. Every question is a content brief with
   demand already proven. Log the recurring ones.
2. **Corrections** — if they are right, thank them and fix it publicly. Doing
   this well converts a critic into an advocate more reliably than anything
   else available.
3. **Substantive disagreement** — engage once, properly. This is where
   credibility is built, in public, with an audience watching.
4. **Enthusiasm** — acknowledge briefly. Volume matters more than depth here.
5. **Bad faith** — do not engage. See below.
6. **Spam and abuse** — remove, do not reply.

The first hour is worth more than the next day combined, for both ranking and
for the tone it sets: a comment section's norms are established by the first
dozen exchanges and are very hard to change afterwards.

## Replying well

- **Answer the question actually asked.** Not the one you wish they asked.
- **Be specific.** "Good point!" is noise. A specific reply is the reason
  people come back, and it is visible to everyone reading.
- **Concede real points immediately and without hedging.** "You're right, I
  got that wrong" ends the exchange and costs nothing. Defending an
  indefensible detail costs the whole thread.
- **Never win publicly against a customer.** Even when correct. Everyone
  reading identifies with the person being corrected, not with the brand.
- **Take it to DM only for genuinely private matters** — account details, a
  refund, personal information. Reflexively saying "DM us" reads as
  deflection and is widely recognized as such.

## Bad faith — identify and stop

Distinguish criticism from bad faith, because they need opposite handling and
the cost of confusing them is high in both directions.

Criticism: specific, about the work, would be satisfied by a fix or an answer.
Bad faith: shifting goalposts, about you rather than the work, escalates when
addressed, and the objection changes each time the previous one is answered.

For bad faith: reply once, factually, for the audience rather than for the
commenter. Then stop. Continuing gives it distribution — on platforms that
rank on reply depth, arguing with a troll is literally promoting them.

Do not delete honest criticism. It gets screenshotted, and the screenshot
travels further than the original ever would have. Delete only spam, abuse,
and material that is illegal or violates the platform's rules.

## Scaling without going cold

- **Template the structure, never the sentence.** Identical replies are
  spotted instantly and read as automation.
- **Answer the top 20, thoroughly, then stop.** Twenty real replies beat two
  hundred emoji.
- **Pin the best answer** so the same question resolves itself.
- **Log recurring questions.** Three occurrences of the same question is a
  piece of content with proven demand. This is the single most reliable
  content pipeline that exists.

## Crisis response

When something has gone genuinely wrong — a real mistake, not merely criticism.

**Speed matters more than polish.** The first hours set the narrative. A
plain, fast acknowledgment beats a perfect statement issued the next day.

**Sequence:**
1. **Acknowledge** — that you have seen it, quickly, even before you know
   everything. "We're aware and looking into it, update within the hour."
2. **Establish facts** — internally, fast. Do not speculate publicly.
3. **Say what happened** — plainly, including the unflattering part. The
   cover-up is always the bigger story.
4. **Say what you are doing** — specifically, with a time.
5. **Follow up** — when you said you would, whether or not it is resolved.

**A real apology has four parts and no fifth:** what happened, that it was
wrong, what you are doing about it, and by when. No explanation of how hard
things have been, no "we're sorry if anyone was offended", no passive voice
hiding who did it. Conditional apologies reliably make things worse and are
recognized instantly.

**When it is not your fault:** say so once, calmly, with evidence, and move
on. Repeated defense reads as guilt regardless of the facts.

**Do not:** delete the original post (it is already screenshotted), argue in
individual replies at scale (it multiplies the surface area), issue a
statement written by committee that says nothing, or go silent for days.

## What not to do at all

- Buy or bait engagement. It attracts an audience that responds to bait, and
  that is the audience you are then stuck with.
- Argue with a large account. You are borrowing their audience's hostility.
- Reply to every negative comment. It signals that negativity gets attention.
- Use a personality-free brand voice in the comments. The comments are the
  one place a human is expected, and its absence is conspicuous.
