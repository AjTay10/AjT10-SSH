# Content atomizer

Producing separately for each platform does not scale and is unnecessary.
Producing once and cross-posting does scale, and does not work — reposted
content reads as reposted, and several platforms detect and demote it
directly.

The middle path: one substantial pillar asset, re-cut natively per platform.

## The pillar

Something with real substance behind it: a long video, a researched article,
a recorded conversation, a talk, a project you actually did. Ten to sixty
minutes of thinking, or 1,500+ words.

The critical property: it must contain **multiple independent ideas**. A
pillar with one idea produces one clip and nine pieces of filler, and the
filler is worse than posting nothing because it dilutes the account's
categorization.

Before atomizing, list the distinct ideas. Fewer than four? The pillar is not
ready — go deeper rather than slicing thinner.

## Extract, in this order

**1. Ideas** — every claim that stands alone without setup. Usually 5–12
in a good pillar. This is the unit of atomization, not "moments".

**2. Moments** — where something happened: a reaction, a demo, a mistake, a
number revealed, a change of mind. These make the best short video.

**3. Artifacts** — anything visual or extractable: a chart, a screenshot, a
framework, a checklist, a before/after, a quote.

**4. Questions** — what the pillar answers, phrased as people actually search.
These become search-facing content with a long tail.

## Map to native formats

Each output is *built for* its platform, not exported to it:

| Source unit | Becomes | Native requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Moment | Short video (TikTok/Reels/Shorts) | New hook in first 1.5s; vertical; captions inside safe area |
| Idea | X post or thread | Standalone claim; no "as I said in my video" |
| Idea + artifact | LinkedIn post | Personal framing; first 2 lines carry it |
| Framework | Carousel (Instagram/LinkedIn) | One idea per slide; slide 1 is the hook |
| Question | YouTube Short / Reddit answer / blog section | Answers completely, on its own |
| Artifact | Pinterest pin | Keyword-bearing title; 2:3; legible as a thumbnail |
| Whole thing | Newsletter | The only place the full argument survives intact |
| Best idea | Community post (Discord/Telegram) | Conversational; invites reply, not consumption |

## The rules that make this work rather than fail

**Every cut needs its own hook.** The pillar's opening does not transfer.
A clip that starts where the moment starts, without a new opening, dies in
the first second. See `hook-craft`.

**Every cut must stand alone.** No "as I mentioned", no "part 3 of", no
reference to the pillar as prerequisite. Assume the viewer has never heard
of you and never will again unless this piece works.

**Strip platform artifacts.** A TikTok watermark on a Reel is demoted —
confirmed, not folklore. Export from the source, never re-download.

**Reformat, do not letterbox.** Horizontal video with black bars top and
bottom in a vertical feed signals lazy repurposing to viewers and to ranking
systems. Recompose the frame.

**Space them out.** Nine cuts in one day competes with itself for the same
audience's attention. Two to four weeks of scheduling from one pillar is
the point — it is what makes the cadence sustainable.

**Vary the angle, not just the format.** Nine cuts of the same idea in nine
shapes is still one post. Nine different ideas is a month of content.

## Realistic yield from one good pillar

```
1 long video (25 min)
├── 4–6 short videos        (one per genuine moment)
├── 3–5 X posts             (one per standalone claim)
├── 2 LinkedIn posts        (the two with a professional angle)
├── 1 carousel              (if there is a framework)
├── 1 newsletter            (the full argument)
├── 2–3 Pinterest pins      (if there are artifacts)
└── 1 Reddit answer         (in the subreddit where the question is asked)

≈ 15 pieces, 3–4 weeks of cadence, one production session.
```

Anything beyond about 15 is padding, and padding is visible.

## Sequencing

Do not lead with the pillar. Post two or three of the strongest cuts *first*
as demand tests — they cost nothing to publish, and if none of them land,
the pillar has a positioning problem worth knowing about before you promote
it. Then release the pillar, then continue the cuts as its long tail.

## What not to atomize

- Anything time-sensitive that will be stale before the cuts run out
- Anything whose value is entirely in its length (a long argument that
  collapses into a slogan is misrepresented by the slogan)
- Anything you are not confident in — atomizing multiplies a mistake by 15
