# YouTube gaming channel: the operator's plan

A business plan for starting a gaming channel from a home PC, written the way
an investor would want it written: base rates first, spend last, and one
strategy struck off the list before anything else.

Grounded in this repo's skills: `platform-playbooks` (YouTube mechanics),
`base-rates` (honest forecasting), `compliance-guard` (rights and platform
terms), `falsify-first` (test before investing), `pre-mortem` (kill criteria).

---

## 0. The strategy that is off the table: reposting other creators' videos

The request was to copy successful YouTubers' videos, modify them, and repost
them. As a business, this is not a gray area and not a shortcut — it is a
strategy with a known, near-100% failure rate, which is why no operator runs
it:

- **Copyright strikes.** Three strikes terminates the channel and every video
  on it. The creators you would copy have teams that file these.
- **Content ID.** Most large channels' footage is fingerprinted. A claim
  doesn't just flag the video — it redirects **all** of its ad revenue to the
  original owner. You do the work; they collect.
- **Reused-content policy.** Even without a strike, YouTube's monetization
  review rejects channels built on "content taken from elsewhere with minimal
  transformation." You can reach 1,000 subscribers on reposts and then be
  denied the ability to earn anything, permanently flagged.
- **"I edited it" and "I credited them" are not defenses.** Fair use is a
  defense argued in a dispute you are already losing, not a permission slip
  (see `compliance-guard`). Attribution is not a license.

The thing worth copying is never the footage — it is the **format**. That is
legal, free, and exactly what every successful channel did. Section 4 covers
how.

## 1. Base rates — read this before spending a dollar

Per `base-rates`: reference class before enthusiasm.

```
Reference class:  new gaming channels, solo creator, standing start,
                  posting consistently for 12 months
Base rate:        low single-digit % reach a self-sustaining audience
                  (estimate, not a measurement — but directionally certain:
                  gaming is YouTube's most supplied niche)
Adjusters:        +  search-first strategy (this plan) — competes on supply
                     gaps, not on charisma against incumbents
                  −  no prior editing/on-camera experience
Implication:      Plan for the 90% case: a year of work and a small channel.
                  The plan below is built so that even the 90% case costs
                  almost nothing and teaches a sellable skill (editing).
```

Two consequences drive everything below:

1. **Spend approaches zero until the idea proves itself.** Anyone selling you
   a $2,000 "streaming setup" before video #1 is the one making money here.
2. **"Go viral" is not a plan** — a viral outcome from a plan whose plan was
   "go viral" rounds to zero. This plan runs on search demand, which is
   boring, measurable, and the one discovery channel where a zero-subscriber
   channel competes on even footing.

## 2. What to buy (and what not to)

You have the PC. That is 90% of the setup. Total additional spend to launch:
**$0.**

### Phase 0 — launch (spend: $0)

| Item | Choice | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Recording/streaming | OBS Studio | Free |
| Editing | DaVinci Resolve (free tier) | Free |
| Microphone | Whatever headset/earbuds you own | $0 |
| Thumbnails | GIMP or Canva free tier | Free |
| Facecam | None — optional for the formats in this plan | $0 |

PC checklist (verify, don't buy):
- An NVIDIA GPU from the GTX 10-series onward (or recent AMD/Intel) has a
  hardware encoder (NVENC/AMF/QSV) — recording costs almost no game
  performance. Set OBS to use it.
- Record at 1080p60 for shooters, 1080p30 is fine for everything else.
- Storage: game capture eats disk fast; 100+ GB free before a session.

### Phase 1 — earned upgrades (spend: ~$60–150, **only after 10 published videos**)

| Item | Why | Rough cost |
|---|---|---|
| USB dynamic microphone + arm | Audio is the only production value viewers actually punish. A dynamic mic rejects room echo and keyboard noise; this is the single highest-ROI purchase in content | $60–120 |
| Second-hand key light or a window | Only if/when you add a facecam | $0–30 |

### Phase 2 — bought with channel revenue, not savings

Camera, capture card (only needed for consoles — PC games need none), second
monitor, acoustic panels. If the channel can't pay for these, the channel is
telling you something. Listen to it.

**The contrarian rule: every gear purchase before proof-of-demand is a way of
feeling productive without publishing.** The graveyard of dead channels is
full of great microphones.

## 3. Streams vs. videos — the part most beginners get backwards

The request was to stream. Here is the skeptical read:

**Do not lead with live streaming.** Live discovery on YouTube (and worse on
Twitch) is nearly nonexistent for new channels — live content is distributed
mostly to existing subscribers, and you have none. Streaming to 0–2 viewers
for months is the most common way this exact plan dies. It also produces the
weakest asset: hours of unedited footage nobody searches for.

**Lead with edited, searchable videos.** YouTube is the only major platform
where search and recommendation both carry real weight (`platform-playbooks`),
and search is the one lever where a day-old channel beats a 5M-sub channel —
by answering a question the big channel didn't bother to.

Sequence:
1. **Months 1–4:** edited long-form (5–12 min) targeting search, plus Shorts
   cut from the best moments (Shorts and long-form share a channel but not an
   algorithm — treat them as two products).
2. **Month 4+:** add a weekly stream *if* videos are pulling 100+ concurrent-
   capable viewers. Streams then do what they're actually good at: deepening
   an audience you already have, and generating raw material to cut into
   videos and Shorts (`content-atomizer`).

## 4. Game selection — where the demand is and the supply isn't

### The trap: copying the big channels' games *and* formats

GTA V, Fortnite, Minecraft funny-moments videos are the most supplied content
on YouTube. A new channel doing what 10,000-subscriber-minimum incumbents do,
with worse editing and no audience, gets zero distribution. Saturation isn't
a reason to avoid a game — it's a reason to avoid the *saturated format* in
that game.

### The play: search-gap content in high-demand games

For each game, the money formats for a zero-audience channel are the ones
people **search**: guides, settings/optimization, "how to fix X", builds and
loadouts, patch breakdowns, money-making methods, tier lists. Unsexy, and the
proven cold-start route.

**Tier 1 — big games, search angle (your GTA/Battlefield instinct, aimed
correctly):**

- **GTA Online** — evergreen search demand for money guides, business setups,
  and weekly-update coverage ("what to buy this week"). Weekly updates =
  built-in recurring topics. ⚠ Two cautions: Rockstar permits monetized
  gameplay content under its fan-content policy, **but GTA's licensed radio
  music triggers Content ID — mute or disable radio while recording.**
- **GTA VI** — currently slated for late 2026 (date has slipped before;
  verify). The largest content opportunity of the decade for this niche.
  Channels that exist *before* launch with GTA Online coverage are positioned
  to own day-one search queries ("GTA VI how to…") while big channels chase
  reaction content. This alone is a reason to start the channel now.
- **Battlefield (current title)** — EA permits monetized gameplay content.
  Best formats: loadout/class guides after each balance patch, settings for
  performance, map-specific tactics. Patch cadence = recurring searchable
  topics.

**Tier 2 — the contrarian picks: dedicated audiences, thinner supply.**
Games with deep systems and recurring resets generate guide demand forever,
and the creator supply is a fraction of GTA's:

- Season/wipe-based games (ARPG seasons, extraction-shooter wipes): every
  reset regenerates search demand for builds and starter guides.
- Newly released or newly updated mid-size games in their first 30 days:
  demand spikes, supply hasn't caught up. Being *early and useful* beats
  being big.
- Sim/strategy/survival games with complex systems: tutorial demand is
  perennial and the audience watch time is long (which YouTube's ranking —
  satisfaction-weighted watch time — pays for directly).

**Rule of thumb:** before committing to a game, search your planned title on
YouTube. If the first page is all million-sub channels answering the question
well, pick a different question. If the first page is thin, outdated, or
low-effort — that's the gap. (Full method: `competitor-recon`, `/gap`.)

### Publisher permission check (once per game — `compliance-guard`)

Most major publishers (Rockstar, EA, Microsoft, Valve) permit monetized
gameplay videos; Nintendo permits it under its Game Content Guidelines with
creative input required. Check the publisher's video policy page once before
building a series on a game, and always kill licensed in-game music.

## 5. How to legally "copy" a successful video, step by step

This is the legitimate version of the repost idea — format modeling. Every
large channel does it.

1. **Find outliers, not hits.** In a niche channel's Videos tab (sorted by
   popular), look for videos with views far above that channel's median —
   e.g. a 40k-view video on a 5k-average channel. That's format demand
   exceeding the creator, which means the *format* works independent of the
   channel. A big channel's hit proves nothing — their subscriber base
   inflates everything.
2. **Deconstruct the packaging.** Title and thumbnail work as a pair posing
   one question (`platform-playbooks`). Write down the question the pair
   creates. That question, not the video, is the asset.
3. **Deconstruct the structure.** Watch it once with a notepad: what happens
   in seconds 0–30 (the steepest retention cliff), how often does it pay off
   a promise, where does it drag.
4. **Make it 100% yours.** Your recording, your script, your voice, your
   examples — with one deliberate improvement: more current (post-patch),
   more complete, better chapters, or a sharper answer. "Same question,
   better answer" is the whole strategy.
5. **Package before you record.** Write the title and sketch the thumbnail
   first. If you can't make the packaging compelling, don't make the video —
   a topic nobody clicks cannot be rescued by editing.
6. **Read the retention graph, not the view count.** A cliff at 0:30 means
   the title promised something the video didn't deliver; a mid-video cliff
   is a segment to cut from the next one. This graph is your entire editing
   education.

What is never yours to use: their footage, their thumbnail image, their exact
title, their script. What is always yours to use: the question they proved
people want answered.

## 6. Income model — ordered by realism, not by fantasy

**Prerequisites for YouTube Partner Program ad revenue** (verify current
terms — they change): 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours in 12
months, or 10M public Shorts views in 90 days. A lower tier (~500 subs)
unlocks fan-funding features earlier. Translation: **months 0–6 income is $0
by design.** Any plan promising otherwise is selling you something.

Revenue streams in the order they'll actually arrive:

1. **Affiliate links** (available day one): gaming peripherals, game key
   retailers. Disclose every time, in the content, per `compliance-guard` —
   "I get a commission if you buy through this link."
2. **Memberships / Super Chat / fan funding** (~500 subs): small but real
   once streams start, because streams convert viewers into patrons.
3. **AdSense** (post-YPP): gaming RPMs are among the lowest on YouTube —
   commonly reported in the low single dollars per 1,000 views (estimate;
   your own analytics will be the first reliable number you have). At that
   rate, ads are a milestone, not a business.
4. **Sponsorships** (10k+ subs, engaged niche): where gaming channels
   actually make money. A niche channel with demonstrable audience trust
   out-earns a bigger channel of passive viewers. Disclose in-video, always.
5. **Own products** (much later): coaching, guides, community — the only
   revenue YouTube can't take away. Convert viewers to an email list from
   day one; every platform audience is rented (`platform-playbooks`).

**The honest financial frame:** treat year one as a paid apprenticeship in
editing, packaging, and analytics — skills with market value even if the
channel lands in the 90% case. The channel is the upside, not the salary.

## 7. The 90-day execution plan

**Cadence: 2 long-form videos per week, 3 Shorts per week, same weekly slots.**
Pick a schedule that survives a bad week (`posting-calendar`) — consistency
you can hold beats ambition you can't.

### Days 1–7: Setup and recon
- [ ] Install OBS + DaVinci Resolve; configure OBS (hardware encoder, 1080p,
      separate audio tracks for game and mic).
- [ ] Record 10 minutes of gameplay, edit 60 seconds of it. This is the real
      test — most people discover here whether they'll actually do this.
- [ ] Pick ONE game and ONE format family (e.g. "GTA Online money/update
      guides"). One. Channels that focus give the algorithm and the audience
      the same clear promise.
- [ ] Run the recon of section 5 on the 5 best-performing small channels in
      that niche. Build a list of 20 proven questions/formats.
- [ ] Check the publisher's content policy. Disable licensed music.

### Days 8–30: First 8 videos — volume over polish
- [ ] Publish 2/week. Package first (title + thumbnail), then record, then
      edit tighter than feels comfortable — cut every second that doesn't
      earn its runtime.
- [ ] First 30 seconds of every video: deliver the promise of the title
      immediately. No channel intro, no "hey guys", no subscribe begging.
- [ ] Cut 3 Shorts/week from long-form material.
- [ ] Expect near-zero views. This phase is skill-building and library-
      building; search content compounds later or not at all.

### Days 31–60: First data
- [ ] Read retention graphs on all videos. Find your personal failure
      pattern (weak packaging? slow openings? mid-video drag?) and fix the
      one biggest issue only.
- [ ] Double down on the 1–2 videos with the best CTR *and* retention —
      make the sequel/adjacent video. Kill formats that failed twice.
- [ ] Keep cadence. 8 more videos.

### Days 61–90: Sharpen
- [ ] 16+ videos published. Compare against tripwires below.
- [ ] If any video found search traction, build a series around it —
      suggested-video pull between your own related videos is the compounding
      loop.
- [ ] Set up the email capture (even a free newsletter) linked in every
      description.

### Tripwires and kill criteria (set now, per `pre-mortem` — not in month 9)

| Signal at day 90 | Read | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Some videos getting steady search impressions, CTR ≥ ~4%, retention improving | The machine works | Continue 90 more days, add streaming at month 4 |
| Views flat but *your* retention/editing measurably improving video-over-video | Skill compounding, topic maybe wrong | Change game/format, keep cadence |
| You've missed cadence 3+ weeks, or dread producing | The honest signal most people ignore | Stop or restructure — this business is a marathon of production, and no tactic fixes not wanting to run it |
| Buying gear instead of publishing | Procrastination with receipts | Publish, then buy |

Review at day 90 against these — written down today so they can't be
renegotiated by hope later (`decision-log`).

## 8. Summary for the impatient

1. Spend $0. Your PC + OBS + a free editor is the whole setup. First
   purchase (a ~$60–120 dynamic mic) comes after video #10.
2. Don't repost anyone's videos. Copy proven *questions*, answer them better,
   in 100% your own footage. Reposts get struck, demonetized, and terminated.
3. Don't stream first. Publish searchable guides in one game — GTA Online
   (radio muted) positioning for GTA VI, Battlefield patch/loadout coverage,
   or a contrarian pick with thin creator supply. Stream after there's an
   audience.
4. Expect $0 for ~6 months; affiliates first, ads are a milestone not a
   business, sponsorships are the business, an email list is the insurance.
5. 2 videos + 3 Shorts a week for 90 days, then judge against the tripwires
   above — not against your hopes.
